# Water filtering basics

Community reference only — not a substitute for local public-health guidance.

Filtering removes **particles**. It does **not** reliably remove viruses or chemicals by itself. Pair filtering with **boiling** or a labeled disinfectant when water may be contaminated.

## When to filter first

- Water is cloudy, muddy, or full of grit.
- You will boil or chemically treat afterward.
- A purpose-built camping/travel filter is available and within its rated life.

## Improvised pre-filter

1. Let water settle in a clean container; pour off the clearer top layer.
2. Pour through a clean cloth, coffee filter, or several layers of tightly woven fabric.
3. Then **boil** (preferred) or follow a disinfectant product label.

## Purpose-built filters

- Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for pore size, flow, and cartridge replacement.
- Keep dirty and clean sides separate; never back-flush dirty water into the clean side.
- Filters rated only for cysts/bacteria still need a disinfection step for many emergency waters.

## Storage after treatment

- Store treated water in clean, food-grade containers with tight lids.
- Keep away from fuels and household chemicals.
- Label the date; rotate on a schedule your site agrees on.

## Do not

- Do not rely on a Brita-style pitcher alone for flood or surface water.
- Do not drink water that smells of fuel or strong chemicals even after filtering.
- Do not mix cleaning chemicals to invent a disinfectant.

If someone becomes severely ill after drinking questionable water, treat it as a medical emergency.
